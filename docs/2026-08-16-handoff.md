## The Iron Beast, in one paragraph

A second M4A1 extra item at 85 packs, mechanically a copy of the Angelic —
same damage multiplier, clip, reserve, stab damage, 3.5 m stab reach, rate of fire,
recoil table and price. It differs in model, sounds, and in having **no awakened
state**: the Angelic model carries two animation sets and switches on a low clip,
this one has seven sequences full stop, so that whole mechanism was removed rather
than adapted. `zp_extra_ironbeast.sma` opens with a list of every difference.

It loads and runs — the log shows it precaching cleanly from 05:44 onward and the
decals resolving — and it has been fired and reloaded in game. Two things have
**not** been checked: buying the Angelic and the Iron Beast in the same round (the
`pev_impulse` keys differ, `748120931` against the Angelic's, so it should be fine,
but "should" is not "was"), and dropping one and picking it back up.

> way is the reason the fix went looking at the model in the first place.

This was the live question and it is about the **Angelic**, not the Iron Beast.

The Angelic alternates between two reload animations - its own comments say seq 4
racks the charging handle while seq 11 thumbs the bolt release - but it schedules
its reload sounds from a **single** pair of constants, `FRAC_CLIPIN 0.39` and
`FRAC_BOLT 0.71`. The two motions do not happen on the same frame, so one animation
is always going to be out of step. As set, the bolt-release animation is right and
the charging-handle pull is audibly late.

Measured, so this is not a pre-roll problem: every Angelic reload sound has its
transient at the very start of the file (`blowback` 0.20 s, `clipin` 0.21 s,
`clipout` 0.16 s, `draw` 0.34 s, onsets all at 0.00-0.04 s). The schedule is simply
late.

The proposed fix, not yet applied:

- turn the two fractions into cvars, the way the Iron Beast's already are, because
  tuning this means watching and nudging and a `#define` costs a recompile and a map
  change per attempt
- give the pull animation and the press animation **separate** fractions, since the
  plugin already knows which one it chose (`g_bAltReload`)

The cheap alternative is to move `FRAC_BOLT` down to about 0.60 and recompile, which
fixes the pull and breaks the press. Ask before doing either - the Angelic is
committed, played and working, and this is a cosmetic defect.

## Two techniques worth reusing

**Measure a sound's envelope instead of guessing where its beat is.** The Iron
Beast's magazine sound is 1.18 s long and it was scheduled as though the impact sat
somewhere in the middle. Reading the waveform showed the peak is in the first
0.08 s and the remaining second is a glass tail decaying to silence. So the useful
number is not "when does the sound play" but "when does the bang land":

    bang time = frac * reload time + transient offset

That reframing is what made the timing solvable rather than a matter of taste.

**Re-time an animation rather than let it be clipped.** `zp_ironbeast_reload_time`
started at the Angelic's 2.1 s while the model's reload sequence was 300 frames at
122 fps = 2.46 s, so the last 0.36 s was cut: the gun went live while the hands were
still moving. Fixed by writing 142.86 fps into the sequence header, which is one
float, so the same 300 frames land on 2.10 s. The Angelic does the same thing at
52 -> 76.2 fps. Originals are kept as `.mdl.orig`.

**If the reload time is ever changed, the model has to be re-timed with it:**
`fps = 300 / time`. Both the cvar and the plugin say so at the point of use.

## Two client cvars the CrossFire packs require

In `userconfig.cfg`, and neither is a matter of taste:

- `gl_max_size 512` — the cap on the texture dimension the renderer will use.
  `config.cfg` already had this right.
- `gl_round_down 0` — at the default of 3 a 512 texture is drawn at 256 and the
  detail is discarded. This was wrong and is why a 512 skin looks soft.

This is global. Every 512 texture in the game benefits, which includes the CSCF
zombie bodies and claws.

`userconfig.cfg` is the right home for client cvars: CS execs it after `config.cfg`
and never writes to it, while `config.cfg` is rewritten from the game's own idea of
the cvars every time it closes.

## Where the CrossFire weapon stats actually live

`game\cstrike\SUIC_SOUNDS\MAIN\AP\SUIC_WEAPON_MAIN.RC` — a weapon config inside a
*sounds* folder. 13 sections including `[M4A1_S_PRISMBEAST]`. `docs/M4A1-S_stats.md`
already had all of it transcribed and read correctly.

Two ways to waste time here, both of which happened:

- **`grep --include=*.rc` does not match `.RC`.** The filter is case sensitive, and
  every one of these files is uppercase, so a search that looks thorough silently
  matches nothing and reports no results rather than an error. Any negative result
  from a filtered grep over this pack should be re-run without the filter.
- **Read `docs/` before searching for facts.** `M4A1-S_stats.md` had been in the
  repo the whole time and was overlooked in favour of hunting for the source file.

For the record, the Prism Beast numbers ended up mattering only for the recoil table
(which the Angelic already used) — everything else was overridden with the Angelic's
values by request, so that document is not the source for the `zp_ironbeast_*` block.

## Assets: what the two downloads each contribute

Neither pack is complete on its own.

| | from |
|---|---|
| viewmodel with **CS default gloves** | `m4a1-s_iron_beast_imperial_gold` |
| world model | `crossfire_m4a1s_ironbeastig` (the only one that ships one) |
| the six sounds | `m4a1-s_iron_beast_imperial_gold` |
| `p_` model | **neither** — `pev_weaponmodel2` points at stock `models/p_m4a1.mdl`, so other players see a plain M4A1 on this player's back |

The larger pack's 20 "choose hands" variants all replace the arms with CrossFire's
own 512x512 sets. The smaller pack's single model is the one textured with CS's own
`view_glove` / `view_skin` / `view_finger` at the stock dimensions, which is why it
is the one installed.

Both models shipped as `v_ak47.mdl` / `w_ak47.mdl`. They were renamed and their
internal name strings corrected — left under the stock names they would have
reskinned every AK in the game.

All six sounds were rewritten as clean 44-byte-header PCM files.
`ClipIn_ImperialGold.wav` carried a `LIST` chunk, and GoldSrc's failure mode for a
WAV it dislikes is silence with no error.

**The sound file names lied about their roles**, and this was only settled by
watching the weapon's showcase video:

| original | what it actually is | installed as |
|---|---|---|
| `G_RELOAD_M4A1.wav` | the **draw**, charging the gun as it comes up | `draw.wav` |
| `ClipIn_ImperialGold.wav` | the magazine going in | `magin.wav` |
| `G_MZC_M4A1_CLIPIN.wav` | the bolt release **after** the magazine | `boltrelease.wav` |

Two of those three were guessed wrong from the file name first. They are installed
under their roles so the name and the moment agree.

## Still open from before

- The Angelic's `INFO` line and every other class's still advertise
  `Knockback++` / `Knockback--` and are now lying: all five are 0.50 and
  `zp_knockback` is 0.
- The HP bump moved Classic and Raptor across a payout boundary, so a kill pays
  them 3 and 2 packs where it used to pay 2 and 1.
- No special ability on the Iron Beast yet. That was deliberately deferred so the
  gun could be played and its animation order checked first. The structure is in
  place to take one.
- A hit marker was investigated and dropped. The downloaded `HitMarkers` plugin
  draws HUD text on an auto channel on every hit, which is exactly the pattern that
  cost a night's work in the previous handoff, and it produces a text symbol rather
  than the red ring that was wanted. The workable approach is a temp-entity sprite
  at the impact point using `sprites/shockwave.spr`, which is a stock Half-Life
  asset every client already has, is additive, and always faces the camera. Nothing
  was installed.
